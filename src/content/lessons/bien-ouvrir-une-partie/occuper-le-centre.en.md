Put a knight in the middle of the board, on e4. Count its squares: eight. Now put it in the corner, on a1. It has two.

That is the whole idea of the centre. The four middle squares — **e4, d4, e5, d5** — are where your pieces see furthest and travel fastest. A piece in the centre works; a piece on the edge waits.

So the two most played first moves in the world are `1.e4` and `1.d4`. They are not fashion: they take a central square with a pawn, and at the same time they open a diagonal for a bishop and air for the queen. One move, three effects.

**What "occupy the centre" does not mean:** pushing every pawn forward. Pawns that go too far cannot come back, and they become targets. Two well-supported pawns in the centre beat four pawns stranded in enemy territory.
